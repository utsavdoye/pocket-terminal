Privacy

What Pocket Terminal knows about you

Short version: nothing. There are no accounts, and no server of ours ever sees your commands, files or screens.

Last updated: 3 November 2026

The app and the program on your computer

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no tracking in the app or the program.
  • Your phone and computer talk directly, end-to-end encrypted. What you type, run, see, download or upload goes only between your own devices.
  • Keys stay on your devices: on the phone, locked with your PIN; on the computer, in ~/.pocket-terminal, readable only by you.
  • The program's log on your computer never records commands, their output or file names.

Third parties your devices talk to

  • A public message board (ntfy.sh by default) passes encrypted notes so your devices can find each other. It sees scrambled text, its size and timing, and your IP address. It can't read the notes.
  • STUN servers (Google, Cloudflare) see your public IP address while the direct link is set up, as in any video call.
  • Phone notifications are delivered by Apple, Google or Mozilla. Each one is encrypted for your phone; they see that a notification was sent, not what it says.
  • The AI helper is off unless you add your own key. Then only what you type in Ask (and, for "Fix with AI", that command and the end of its output) goes to the provider you chose, under their privacy policy.

This website

We count visits with GoatCounter: no cookies, no personal data, no tracking across sites, just page views, referrers and rough country, so we know whether anyone reads this. The live star and install counts come from GitHub and npm.

Open source

All of it is open source under the MIT license, so you can check every claim on this page in the code. The security page explains exactly what an attacker could and couldn't do.
